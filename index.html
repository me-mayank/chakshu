01Executive summary

CHAKSHU is a public platform that improves passenger safety through crowdsourced incident reporting and vehicle-specific safety intelligence.

Passengers search a vehicle by registration number and review previously submitted safety reports. Passengers who experience uncomfortable, suspicious, intimidating, harassing or unsafe behaviour can submit a structured report, which feeds a shared repository that helps future passengers decide before they board.

Unlike booking, navigation, payment and rating platforms, CHAKSHU focuses on recurring safety concerns tied to individual vehicles. It does not establish criminal guilt, certify vehicles as safe or unsafe, or replace law enforcement. It makes community-reported safety information accessible, structured, transparent and useful.

02Problem statement

Current landscape

Passengers rely on independently operated autos, e-rickshaws, taxis and app-based cabs, yet know little about earlier safety experiences with a given vehicle. Existing services offer:

  • Driver ratings and reviews
  • Provider-supplied vehicle and driver details
  • Trip information and tracking
  • Complaints confined to a single platform

Limitations

  • Safety experiences stay locked inside individual booking platforms.
  • Independently hired transport has no accessible safety history.
  • Passengers cannot warn future passengers.
  • Recurring behavioural patterns go unnoticed.
  • No universal, community-maintained repository exists across providers.

Core problem

There is no sufficiently accessible, independent, community-driven mechanism for passengers to check previously reported safety concerns about a vehicle before boarding it.

03Product vision

Establish a community-powered transportation safety intelligence network where passengers contribute, discover and access vehicle-specific safety information, turning isolated experiences into collective, preventive knowledge.

Mission

Enable passengers to:

  • Check the available safety history of a vehicle
  • Report concerning experiences
  • Identify recurring safety patterns
  • Make informed transport decisions
  • Contribute to a shared safety network

Guiding principles

  • Prevention over reaction. Surface concerning information before another incident occurs.
  • Collective intelligence. Individual experiences build community awareness.
  • Privacy by design. Protect reporters’ identities and personal data.
  • Evidence-conscious reporting. Separate allegations, reviewed reports and independently supported information.
  • Fairness and accountability. Protect passengers while shielding drivers from unchecked or fabricated claims.

04Users and personas

A. Safety-conscious passenger

Regular auto, taxi or cab user who wants to know whether concerns have been reported.

  • Search a registration number fast
  • See nature and recency of reports
  • Decide before boarding

B. Reporting passenger

Has experienced uncomfortable, threatening or inappropriate behaviour.

  • Report conveniently
  • Stay publicly unidentified
  • Receive confirmation and understand moderation

C. Community contributor

Documents genuine experiences to build a reliable database.

  • Submit structured reports
  • Avoid duplicates
  • Read transparent policies

D. Reported vehicle operator

Finds reports associated with their vehicle.

  • Understand published concerns
  • Challenge wrong attribution or fabricated reports
  • Request review and fair resolution

06Functional requirements

6.1 Vehicle safety search

Search by Indian registration number is the platform’s primary entry point.

  • Search and public safety information require no account.
  • Registration numbers are normalised before lookup, supporting applicable Indian formats.
  • Results match the exact normalised registration number.
  • Invalid formats return a clear validation message.
  • Results never expose reporter identities.
Result stateBehaviour
AReports availableShow total and published report counts, incident categories, dates, moderation status, evidence indicators and dispute information.
BNo reports foundState that no safety reports have been submitted on the platform yet. Absence of reports must never be presented as proof of safety.
CUnder reviewReports exist but have not completed moderation. Pending submissions must be visibly distinct from published reports.

6.2 Vehicle safety profile

Every vehicle with at least one report has a consolidated profile showing: registration number and verification status; total and published report counts; incident category distribution; submission dates and recent activity; evidence indicators; moderation information; and dispute status.

The community safety indicator must favour descriptive categories over numerical scores, must not imply official verification or guilt, and must rest on published criteria covering report quality, recency, independence, severity and moderation status. A vehicle with no reports never receives a “certified safe” status.

6.3 Incident reporting

Authenticated passengers submit structured reports through a simple flow designed to minimise friction for people who may be distressed.

Authentication

  • Google sign-in is mandatory to report.
  • Each account gets an internal platform identity.
  • Google account details never appear publicly.
  • A mobile number is never required.

Required fields

  • Vehicle registration number
  • Incident category
  • Incident date or approximate date
  • Incident description

Optional fields

  • Approximate journey location
  • Photos or documents
  • Ride receipt or booking reference
  • Additional context

  • Reports attach to a specific vehicle record; a new record is created if none exists.
  • All reports are moderated before publication, and duplicates are detected.
  • Separate incidents involving the same vehicle may be reported.
  • Submitting a report never triggers an automatic negative classification.

6.4 Reporter identity and privacy

Create enough accountability to deter malicious reports without exposing passengers.

  • Unique internal user ID per account; Google identifiers handled securely.
  • Identity data stays separate from public reports.
  • Names, emails and account IDs never appear on public profiles or public APIs.
  • Collect only necessary data; restrict access to identity records.
  • Define retention and deletion policies and publish clear privacy disclosures.
  • Handle lawful disclosure requests under applicable law.

The platform offers privacy protection without promising absolute anonymity.

6.5 Duplicate reporting prevention

Core rule: one account may submit one initial report per vehicle.

  • Device identifiers and IP rate limiting serve only as supplementary abuse signals.
  • Substantially repeated submissions are detected.
  • Genuinely new incidents may be reported separately.
  • Multiple accounts or devices are not assumed to be independent witnesses.
  • Suspicious patterns are routed to moderation.

The platform does not claim to guarantee one physical device per report, since browsers cannot reliably identify hardware.

6.6 Report moderation

Reports never become public allegations on submission. Each report enters a pending state and is checked for abusive, irrelevant, defamatory or identifying content, with personal information redacted and evidence reviewed for relevance and privacy. A report can be approved, rejected or returned for clarification; every decision is recorded, and published reports stay subject to later review.

Launch-stage moderation model

No dedicated moderation team is assumed at launch. Moderation relies on automation plus a small admin function that can grow later.

  • Automated pre-screening. Flags abusive language, phone numbers, names and other personal data, and duplicate or suspicious submissions, before a human sees the report.
  • Admin review. The founder or a small admin group works the review queue and makes the approve, reject or clarify decision.
  • Conservative publication. Nothing is published without admin approval. A slow queue is acceptable; a careless one is not.
  • Community flagging. Signed-in users can flag published reports for re-review.
  • Growth path. Trained volunteers or safety-organisation partners can join as reviewers as volume grows, under the same audit logging.

6.7 Dispute and appeal

Affected vehicle operators can challenge incorrect or fabricated reports.

  • A dispute identifies the relevant report and may include supporting documents.
  • An authorised administrator reviews it.
  • Outcomes are recorded and corrections are reflected on the public profile.
  • A dispute neither removes a report automatically nor counts as an admission of wrongdoing.

6.8 Community safety intelligence

Reports are consolidated into patterns, not tallies of negative reactions. Factors weighed:

  • Independent report count, recency, nature and severity
  • Similarity between descriptions
  • Supporting evidence and moderation outcomes
  • Duplicate or coordinated reporting signals
  • Dispute history

Intelligence principles

  • One unverified allegation never creates an elevated-risk classification.
  • Repeated similar reports are a stronger signal than unrelated ones.
  • Old reports are weighted below recent ones.
  • Rejected or removed reports never feed public indicators.
  • Unverified and reviewed reports are clearly distinguished.
  • The indicator methodology is public.

07User journeys

Vehicle search

  1. Passenger opens the platform
  2. Enters a registration number
  3. System validates and normalises it
  4. Community database is queried
  5. Passenger receives result state A, B or C
  6. Profile is shown, or absence of records is communicated
  7. Passenger searches another vehicle

Incident reporting

  1. Selects “report vehicle”
  2. Signs in with Google
  3. Enters or confirms registration number
  4. Existing vehicle record is selected or a new one created
  5. Selects incident category
  6. Provides description and details
  7. Duplicate and abuse checks run
  8. Report goes to moderation
  9. Passenger receives confirmation
  10. Report is published or rejected under policy

Vehicle dispute

  1. Operator opens the dispute facility
  2. Identifies the report
  3. States reasons and adds supporting information
  4. Administrator reviews
  5. Decision is recorded
  6. Public status updated where appropriate

08Non-functional requirements

Security

  • Credentials handled securely; sensitive data encrypted
  • Abuse prevention on public endpoints
  • Role-based admin access
  • Evidence files private by default
  • Protection against common web vulnerabilities

Privacy

  • No public exposure of personal data
  • Purpose limitation and data minimisation
  • Defined retention and clear disclosures
  • Compliance with Indian data-protection requirements

Availability and performance

  • Low-latency search, no login needed
  • Scales with report volume
  • Profiles stay available in normal operation
  • Errors never leak sensitive information

Accessibility

  • Mobile-first, responsive
  • Clear, plain language
  • Accessible forms and validation
  • Minimal steps to search
  • Reporting usable at any technical literacy

09Administrative requirements

At launch this role may be held by the founder or a small admin group. Authorised administrators must be able to:

  • Review reports and supporting evidence
  • Approve or reject reports
  • Identify suspicious submission patterns
  • Manage vehicle records and correct wrong associations
  • Handle disputes and appeals
  • Remove prohibited personal information
  • Review user abuse reports
  • Suspend or restrict abusive accounts
  • Maintain moderation audit logs

Every administrative action affecting published safety information must be auditable.

10Safety, legal and ethical considerations

CHAKSHU handles allegations about real people and vehicles. Its credibility depends on balancing public safety with fairness, privacy and responsible publication.

10.1 No determination of guilt

The platform never declares a driver guilty of a crime on community submissions alone. Reports are passenger-submitted experiences and allegations, not judicial findings.

10.2 Vehicle identity limits

At launch, registration numbers are community-submitted and not verified against the RTO database. A valid format does not prove the physical vehicle displaying that number is genuine. The platform must say so clearly.

10.3 False and malicious reporting

Reasonable safeguards must cover personal disputes, deliberate false allegations, coordinated campaigns, incorrect registration numbers, duplicate incidents and attempts to expose personal information.

10.5 Regulatory compliance

Consider Indian law on personal data protection, defamation and intermediary responsibilities, cybersecurity, consumer protection, information technology and lawful information requests. Publication and moderation policies need review before public launch.

10.4 EMERGENCY DISCLAIMER
CHAKSHU is not an emergency response service. Anyone in immediate danger should be directed to emergency assistance, including India’s emergency number 112.

11Success metrics

Success is measured by the platform’s ability to deliver useful safety information within a trustworthy reporting environment.

Adoption

  • Registered users
  • Unique vehicle searches
  • Vehicles in the database
  • Returning-user rate
  • Search-to-report conversion

Reporting

  • Reports submitted and approved
  • Average moderation time
  • Reports with evidence
  • Duplicate submission rate

Trust and quality

  • Rejection rate in moderation
  • Successful dispute resolutions
  • Reports corrected or removed
  • Coordinated-reporting detection rate
  • User-reported confidence

Safety utility

  • Searches returning useful information
  • Profiles viewed before journeys
  • User-reported usefulness
  • Searches leading to informed follow-up action

A decline in reported incidents must not be the only success measure. Rising reports may reflect better awareness and access, not worsening safety.

12Assumptions and constraints

  • Passengers can identify vehicles by registration number.
  • Users are willing to contribute genuine safety experiences.
  • Google authentication gives reasonable initial account-level accountability.
  • Properly moderated community reports can form a useful repository.
  • Government RTO API access is not required for the initial product, which runs on user-submitted registration numbers.
  • Useful vehicle-level information can be built without collecting unnecessary driver identity data.
  • Safety indicators must account for incomplete data and uncertainty.
  • Early moderation volume can be handled by automated screening and a small admin group, with reviewers added as volume grows.

14Acceptance criteria

The initial product is functionally complete when:

  • A passenger can search a vehicle without an account.
  • Vehicles with reports are distinguished from those without.
  • A passenger can authenticate via Google before reporting.
  • An authenticated passenger can submit a structured report.
  • Reports attach to the correct vehicle record.
  • Duplicate initial submissions by one account against one vehicle are blocked.
  • Separate genuine incidents can be submitted and told apart.
  • Reports are moderated before publication.
  • Public profiles never expose reporter identities.
  • Users can tell unverified from reviewed information.
  • Vehicle operators have a dispute mechanism.
  • Missing reports are never presented as proof of safety.
  • Administrative actions are auditable.
  • Appropriate privacy and safety disclosures are provided.

15Conclusion

CHAKSHU adds a layer of transparency and preventive awareness to everyday transport. Its contribution is not booking, driver discovery or conventional ratings, but converting individual passenger experiences into structured, accessible, community-maintained safety information. The long-term vision is a trusted, independent safety intelligence network that complements existing transport services and contributes to safer public mobility.

Product north star

Make relevant community-reported safety information accessible before a passenger boards a vehicle, while protecting the privacy and rights of everyone involved.
